microservices are **not automatically better**. They introduce new problems:
```text
network failure
service-to-service communication
distributed transactions
data consistency
message duplication
service discovery
monitoring
deployment complexity
```
That is why I recommend you learn microservices in this order:
1. **Monolith first** — understand why splitting is needed.
2. **What is a service boundary?** — decide what belongs to User, Trip, Payment, etc.
3. **Service-to-service communication** — REST first.
4. **Database per service** — why services should not freely touch each other's tables.
5. **Async communication** — Kafka/RabbitMQ.
6. **Distributed data consistency** — Saga, eventual consistency.
7. **Reliability** — timeout, retry, circuit breaker, idempotency.
8. **Infrastructure** — Docker, API Gateway, service discovery, Kubernetes/cloud.
9. **Observability** — logs, metrics, tracing.
    For your RideNow project, it's actually a very good system for learning microservices because it naturally creates problems like **multiple drivers accepting one trip, realtime location, async events, payment, and consistency across services**.  
    I suggest we learn it from the beginning like this:
```text
MAIN PROBLEM
"What problem does microservice solve?"
        ↓
Problem 1
Why is one big monolith sometimes difficult?
        ↓
Problem 2
How do we decide where to split services?
        ↓
Problem 3
How do services talk to each other?
        ↓
Problem 4
What happens when one service fails?
        ↓
Problem 5
How do we keep data consistent?
```
Start with just this sentence:
> **Microservices are not about making many small projects. They are about separating business responsibilities so parts of the system can evolve, deploy, and scale independently.**  
> Next, the most useful thing to learn is **why a monolith becomes a problem and when you should NOT use microservices**.
